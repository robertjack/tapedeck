# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the work, not with the person who brought it.

## What that means here

Everyone taking part in this project — issues, pull requests, discussions,
anywhere else it happens — is expected to:

- Assume the other person is acting in good faith, especially when they're
  wrong.
- Criticize ideas, code, and specifications freely and directly. Disagreement is
  the point of a review; contempt is not.
- Accept that "no" is a normal outcome. A maintainer declining a change isn't an
  insult, and a contributor pushing back on a decision isn't insubordination.
- Say what you actually observed. Bug reports, failure output, and honest
  accounts of what broke are more valuable than reassurance.

The following are not acceptable, in any volume or tone:

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks — including about someone's identity,
  background, or level of experience.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, bad-faith argument, or wearing people
  down until they leave.

## Scope

This applies in every space this project occupies, and to anyone representing
the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report anything that needs attention by opening an issue, or privately to
[@robertjack](https://github.com/robertjack) on GitHub. Reports are handled
discreetly, and the reporter's identity is not disclosed to the person reported.

The maintainer may warn, edit or remove contributions, or block accounts —
proportionate to what happened, and with the reasoning stated where doing so
doesn't expose the reporter.
